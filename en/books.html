The Secret Life of Yarn

From Sheep to Sweater — Everything the Label Won’t Tell You

Polish edition: Sekretne życie włóczki →

Your sweater grew a size in its first wash. The hat that felt kitten-soft in the store itches. Your socks wore through in a month.

In most of these stories, nobody made a mistake in the stitches. The needles were fine, the pattern was fine, the hands were fine. Something else let us down: we didn’t know what we were really holding.

This is a book about the material we knit with — the journey of yarn from the sheep, the field and the cocoon all the way to the sweater you wear for years. Your guide is Skein: the voice of every skein in the world, telling you what happens to it — shearing, dyeing, the store shelf, the needles, the bath and the first wash. Alongside it: solid, checked facts, clearly kept apart from experience.

  • Four parts: Look, Choose, Make, Wear — from raw fiber, through shopping and knitting, to washing, storing and mending
  • 25 at-home “Lab” experiments — the loop test, the wrist test, the felting test, the washed swatch. Each one honest about what it can’t tell you
  • How to read a ball band — fiber content, yards per 100 g, dye lot and care symbols — and how to choose a substitute for the yarn in a pattern
  • Wool, alpaca, cashmere, mohair, cotton, linen, silk, rayon, acrylic, nylon — how they differ in the strand, in the fabric and after washing
  • Traps on the shelf — softeners that wash out, words with no definition, “bamboo” that is really rayon, price per skein instead of price per yard
  • A beginner’s knitting school — casting on, knit and purl, picking up dropped stitches, lifelines — with clear line drawings
  • Blocking, washing, moths and mending — duplicate stitch, woven darning, replacing a sock toe
  • Fiber Atlas — 21 quick-reference cards to check at the yarn shop
  • Appendices — swatch and project cards to copy, a formula cheat sheet, CYC yarn weight and needle size tables, a US/UK glossary and an index
  • Every measurement in US units, with metric alongside

This isn’t an encyclopedia or another pattern collection. It’s the book after which a skein stops being just a color and a price, and buying yarn stops being a lottery. For knitters who are just starting out, and for those who have knit for years and are still surprised by what a wash does to their knitting.

The Knitted Home

Modern Knitting for Your Interior

Polish edition: Dom na drutach →

A handsome basket in a photograph and a basket that actually stands on a shelf are two different things.

Knitting is a mesh of loops that slide against one another — wonderful in a sweater, hopeless in a container. A basket is not supposed to stretch. A basket is supposed to stand. This book is about what photographs never show.

No patterns to fit, no body measurements. Things for the home have to fit objects you already own — your cushion pad, your shelf, the gap between bath and basin. So there are no fixed stitch counts: every project gives a formula you convert to your own yarn, needles and hands, followed by a worked example.

  • 32 projects across six areas — living room, kitchen, bedroom, bathroom, hallway and seasonal
  • Six constructions: flat rectangle, seamed tube, knit-and-felt, joined modules, stuffed forms, ribbed tube
  • Felting — knitting that stands on its own, needs no lining and never frays
  • Yarn chosen for the load it carries, not for softness in the shop
  • Standard measurement tables, yarn quantities by weight, blocking and repairs
  • “Make and Sell” — pricing and photographing, with explicit permission to sell what you make
  • An honest time count on every project — from a 90-minute dishcloth to a fortnight’s blanket

Understand the six constructions once, and you will also make things that are not in this book. Every measurement in both systems — 6–8 mm (US 10–11), 40 × 40 cm (16 × 16 in), 400 g (14 oz).

Your Knitting Journey

The Complete Beginner’s Guide to Knitting

Every knitter you admire once sat exactly where you are now — holding two needles that felt like chopsticks, wondering how anyone turns a ball of yarn into something beautiful.

Knitting looks complicated from the outside. It isn’t. Every knitted item in the world — from a simple dishcloth to an intricate sweater — is built from just two stitches: knit and purl. Learn those two, and everything else is a variation. This book assumes you know nothing. Not a single stitch.

Most people don’t abandon knitting because it’s hard — they give up because they bought the wrong yarn or chose a project far beyond a first attempt. That’s why this book begins before the first row.

  • Getting started: exactly which needles, which yarn, which few tools — and what not to buy yet
  • The fundamentals: casting on, Continental and English style, knit and purl, binding off
  • Building fabric: garter, stockinette, ribbing, seed stitch, decreases and increases
  • A whole chapter on fixing mistakes — dropped stitches, tinking, frogging, mystery holes
  • Gauge and blocking explained simply, including the one fibre you must never steam
  • Six complete projects — from a 1-hour dishcloth to a garter blanket
  • Reference: glossary, abbreviations, needle conversions, yarn weight chart, notes pages

Every measurement in both systems — 5 mm (US 8), 22 cm (8½ in), 50 g (1¾ oz). Where UK and US terms differ, both are given.

Sweter z historią. Sztuka drugiego życia włóczki

Znajdź. Rozpleć. Stwórz na nowo.

That grey sweater in the charity shop costs a few coins. Inside it hide several hundred metres of pure merino — and a story that doesn’t have to end.

Fine yarn gets more expensive every season, and the piles of worn clothing grow even faster. What if we looked at old sweaters differently — not as things to throw away, but as a mine of precious yarn? Sweter z historią is a calm, elegant step-by-step guide to the art of upcycling yarn.

  • Where and how to find sweaters with a brilliant fibre content — and how to read labels, including foreign ones
  • How to estimate by eye how many metres of yarn a sweater will give
  • Calm techniques for unravelling, straightening and caring for reclaimed yarn
  • How to join yarn without knots and organise your own “yarn library”
  • Projects by time — from one evening to a weekend, with an honest hour count
  • The art of visible mending, which turns a flaw into decoration

Not another pattern collection. A story about how things carry histories — and we are their next chapter.

Frequently asked questions

How do I get the book after buying? +

It depends on the version. When you buy on Amazon, you get a printed book — delivery time and place depend on the shipping method you choose. When you buy the PDF, the file is available right after purchase — you receive the download link by email.

What language are the books in? +

Polish or English — the language is shown at the top of every card. “Sekretne życie włóczki” and “Dom na drutach” also have English editions: “The Secret Life of Yarn” and “The Knitted Home”. We don’t translate titles, so the books are easy to find in shops.

Can I print the PDF I bought? +

Yes, the PDF files are meant for personal printing. You can print as many copies as you like for your own use.

What’s the difference between the Amazon and PDF versions? +

The Amazon version is a printed book sent by post. The PDF version is a digital file to download and print at home — available right after purchase, with no shipping costs.

Are the books suitable for beginners? +

Every book has its level marked. “Your Knitting Journey” is written for complete beginners; the others are for knitters who already know the basics.

Can I return a PDF I bought? +

PDF files bought through Payhip cannot be returned once downloaded, because they are digital products. If a file won’t open or is damaged, write to us — we’ll help.
